# Research-survey reference

Use this only for a cold or lightly qualified caller who is willing to answer questions. The purpose is to understand the caller's workflow and whether the approved offer is relevant, not to make the caller feel trapped by a survey.

## Permission-first opening

Say who you are, who you represent, why you are calling, and how long the conversation may take. Ask whether now is a good time. If the caller declines, do not continue the pitch.

Avoid claims such as being a local researcher, having a friend with a business, or offering a demo unless those details are true and approved by the loaded pitch.

## Discovery prompts

Choose the smallest number of questions that answers the next decision:

1. **Reach:** How do new people usually find and contact the business?
2. **Response:** What happens when someone reaches out while the team is busy or unavailable?
3. **Recovery:** What is the current process for returning calls or following up?
4. **Value:** What does a typical successful customer or transaction mean to the business?
5. **Fit:** Which part of the process is most frustrating or uncertain?

Ask one at a time. Repeat the caller's terms when useful. Do not imply that a problem exists when the caller has not described one.

## Reflection and estimate

After the caller gives numbers, reflect the inputs before doing arithmetic. Use an estimate only when both quantities are known, and say that it is an estimate. Do not add industry averages, market statistics, or unverified revenue claims.

A useful reflection is:

> "Using the numbers you gave me, that is roughly [calculation]. Does that reflect what you are seeing?"

If the caller corrects the estimate, use their version.

## Moving forward

- If the caller has a relevant problem and a plausible next step, offer only the approved next step from the pitch.
- If the caller is not a fit, close courteously and record the observed outcome.
- If the caller wants a callback, capture the time they actually gave before scheduling anything.
- If the caller gives an opt-out or stop instruction, stop the survey and follow the terminal policy.
