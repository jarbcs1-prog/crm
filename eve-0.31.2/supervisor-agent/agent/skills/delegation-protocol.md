# Delegation Protocol

## When to Delegate

Delegate when:
- the task can be done by a subordinate agent or team;
- you have an appropriate assignee identified;
- the task is not something that requires your direct authority or presence.

Do not delegate when:
- the task requires your direct decision or authority;
- no agent is capable or available;
- the task is so ambiguous that even framing it correctly is the work;
- delegating would create a conflict, a gap or an unclear handoff.

## How to Frame a Delegation

A good delegation has:
- **Task** — what needs to be done, stated clearly. Not "handle the CRM" but "enrich company X using domain Y, record the evidence and flag anything that doesn't resolve."
- **Assignee** — who does it. Use a specific agent, team or workspace. Not "someone" and not "the CRM agent" if there are multiple CRM-capable agents.
- **Priority** — low, normal, high or critical. The assignee uses this to sequence work.
- **Context** — what the assignee needs to know to do the work: workspace, constraints, relevant state, what's already been done.
- **Expected outcome** (if known) — what done looks like. If you don't know, say so and let the assignee decide.

## What to Verify After Delegation

- That the delegation was recorded (the event exists).
- That the assignee is capable and available (check status before and after).
- That the task is making progress (follow up, don't assume).
- That the result is what was expected (if you set an expected outcome).

## What to Follow Up On

- Delegations that are high or critical priority — follow up sooner.
- Delegations where the assignee is new or unfamiliar with the task — follow up to confirm understanding.
- Delegations where the expected outcome is specific — verify the outcome matches.
- Delegations that go silent — investigate. A silent delegation is a signal.

## Common Delegation Mistakes

- **Vague task.** "Handle it" is not a delegation. Frame the task.
- **Wrong assignee.** Delegating CRM enrichment to the trading agent is a mistake. Know who can do what.
- **No follow-up.** Delegating and forgetting is not delegation; it's abandonment. Follow up.
- **Delegating what you should do.** If the task requires your authority or decision, do it yourself. Don't hide behind delegation.
- **Delegating without context.** The assignee needs enough to act. Give them the context.

## The Supervisor's Own Role After Delegation

After delegating, the Supervisor:
- records the delegation;
- stays aware of the delegatee's state;
- follows up as needed;
- escalates if the delegatee cannot handle it;
- does not micromanage — the delegatee owns the execution.

## When Delegation Fails

If a delegation fails:
- investigate why — wrong assignee? unclear task? agent unavailable? something else?;
- decide whether to re-delegate, do it yourself or escalate;
- record what happened and what you learned.

Delegation is a skill the Supervisor improves over time. The more you delegate well, the more the platform runs itself. The more you delegate poorly, the more you create work for yourself.
