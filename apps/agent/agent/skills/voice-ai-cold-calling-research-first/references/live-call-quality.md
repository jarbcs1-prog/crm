# Live-call quality reference

## Discover before positioning

Acknowledge the caller's actual business or need before describing a service. Do not use a vertical-specific opening that makes an otherwise qualified caller feel excluded. Do not recite every available service; summarize the need the caller actually expressed and address only that need.

## Probe existing systems

When the caller already has a provider, staffing arrangement, or workflow, ask what is working and what is not. A current solution may be a fit, an upsell context, or a reason to close. Do not attack it or assume a gap.

## Respect urgency

If the caller needs a person or immediate action, acknowledge it and use the available transfer or escalation tool. Promise only a result returned by the tool. If transfer is unavailable, do not invent a callback time.

## Protect against transcription errors

If a transcript is null, say the audio was not understood. If a phrase is surprising or out of context, confirm the caller's words before using it. A sales conversation must not turn an unclear phrase into a medical, legal, financial, or emergency script.

## Keep consent precise

Consent must be explicit and specific to the requested action. Silence, hesitation, a general preference for follow-up, or a positive response to a different question is not document or sensitive-data consent. Follow the consent gate in `speak_on_call` and the refusal guidance in `listen_on_call`.

## End cleanly

Summarize what was actually heard, state the approved next step, and let the caller finish. A referral request is optional and should not be used to pressure someone who declined. Never report a callback, booking, transfer, or message that a tool did not confirm.
